# Bradley & Lang — Affective Norms for English Words (ANEW) (1999) — Research Findings

## Citation

Bradley, M. M., & Lang, P. J. (1999). *Affective norms for English words (ANEW): Instruction manual and affective ratings.* Technical Report C-1, The Center for Research in Psychophysiology, University of Florida.

Local source (primary, full text with raw all-subjects/male/female rating tables): `research/tone-taxonomy-v2/sources/anew-1999/Bradley-Lang-1999-ANEW.pdf`

## Scope & Relevance

ANEW directly informs **valence, arousal, dominance** — the three dimensions it was built to measure, using the Self-Assessment Manikin (SAM) on a 1–9 scale (5 = neutral), following the Osgood/Mehrabian–Russell dimensional lineage cited in the brief. Norms are reported for ~1,034 words, each rated by a balanced sample of University of Florida introductory psychology students (all-subjects, male-only, and female-only tables are all provided).

ANEW is **silent** on `warmth`, `tension`, `intimacy`, `instability`, `nostalgia`, `beauty`, and `menace` as named constructs — it has no rating scale for any of them. Any use of ANEW for those seven dimensions is necessarily indirect: either (a) borrowing the V/A/D profile of a word that happens to overlap semantically with the dimension (e.g., using "menace" the noun's V/A/D as a proxy for the `menace` dimension), or (b) using nearby synonyms when the exact V1 descriptor isn't in the corpus. This is flagged per-row below. For `nostalgia` specifically, ANEW has **zero** usable evidence — the concept of retro/wistful memory is outside its 1999 word list and outside the VAD framework entirely.

Only 7 of the 20 current V1 descriptors are exact matches in ANEW's word list: `subdued`, `cold`, `tense`, `relaxed`, `safe`, `beautiful`, `intimate`. The remaining 13 required proxy words or have no support at all.

## Candidate New/Renamed Descriptors

ANEW does not suggest new *dimensions* (it only measures V/A/D), but it does surface several exact-match, high-confidence words that could **add** to the V1 vocabulary as secondary descriptors within existing dimensions, since exact ANEW matches are inherently more trustworthy anchors than the proxy words above:

- **add** `sad` (valence-, n=26) — cleaner, more universally-recognized synonym for `melancholic`, and it has a direct ANEW rating whereas `melancholic` does not.
- **add** `joyful` or `hopeful` (valence+) — direct-rated alternatives/companions to `uplifting`.
- **add** `aroused` or `vigorous` (arousal+) — direct-rated alternatives to `energetic`; caution that `aroused` carries sexual/romantic connotations in casual usage and may need a media-safe label.
- **add** `hostile` or `danger` (menace+) — direct-rated, stronger anchors than `threatening` for the menace dimension.
- **add** `powerful` or `strong` (dominance+) — direct-rated, more common descriptors than `commanding`.
- **add** `peace`/`peaceful` (tension-) — direct-rated (V7.72/A2.95/D5.45), a strong companion/alternative to `relaxed`.
- **add** `chaos` (instability+) — direct-rated, reasonable anchor for the instability dimension even though "unstable" itself is unrated.
- **rename** consideration: `commanding` → `powerful` (has a direct ANEW rating and is more colloquial for media tone tagging; `commanding` does not appear in the corpus at all).
- **rename** consideration: `threatening` → `hostile` or `dangerous` (both closer to directly-attested ANEW vocabulary than "threatening").

## Conflicts / Cautions

- **Coverage gap**: only 7/20 V1 descriptors (`subdued`, `cold`, `tense`, `relaxed`, `safe`, `beautiful`, `intimate`) are exact ANEW matches. The other 13 rely on proxy words of varying quality, and `nostalgic`/`unsentimental` have zero support — ANEW cannot anchor the nostalgia dimension at all.
- **`cold` arousal is near-neutral (5.19)**, not low — undermines any assumption that "cold" implies "low energy." The valence-based cold/warm metaphor holds, but the temperature→arousal intuition does not, per this lexicon.
- **`intimate` has surprisingly high arousal (6.98)** — ANEW's raters likely associated "intimate" partly with romantic/sexual arousal rather than quiet emotional closeness. If V2 intends `intimate` to also describe hushed, low-energy closeness (e.g., a quiet conversation scene), this ANEW-derived arousal secondary mapping should not be trusted without corroboration from a media-specific source (e.g., LIRIS-ACCEDE).
- **`distant` proxies (`alone`, `lonely`) are far more negative in valence (avg V=3.16)** than what V1 intends for a neutral/cool "distant" tone descriptor — `alone`/`lonely` connote sadness/isolation, not mere emotional distance. Recommend sourcing a cleaner anchor (e.g., `detached`, `aloof` in isolation) from Warriner or NRC instead.
- **Arousal scale compression**: across the full ANEW corpus, very few words exceed arousal ratings of ~8 (e.g., "orgasm" 8.10, "rage" 8.17, "win" 8.38 for males). A simple linear `(v-5)/4` normalization likely *underestimates* the intended strength of high-arousal descriptors like `energetic`, `tense`, `threatening`. Recommend a non-linear rescale (e.g., min-max normalize within ANEW's own arousal range, or z-score) before committing final V2 weights.
- **Gender-split divergence**: ANEW provides separate male/female tables, and some emotionally/sexually loaded words diverge substantially by gender (e.g., `erotic`: male V=8.16 vs. female V=6.73; `rape`: male V=1.70 vs. female V=1.08; `enraged`: male A=7.90/D=7.70 vs. female A=8.00/D=5.86). This is a caution for any menace- or intimacy-adjacent word — the all-subjects average may mask meaningfully different affective readings by rater demographic, which is itself a limitation worth noting given the "culturally loaded terms" concern in the brief (ANEW's raters were 1990s US undergraduates only).
- **Correlated proxy words risk dimension bleed**: `cruel`, `brutal`, `hostile` (used as `harsh`/`menace` proxies) carry low valence + low dominance simultaneously, so using them to anchor multiple dimensions (`beauty`-negative, `menace`, `dominance`-negative) risks the same underlying signal being double-counted across dimensions in V2's weighted-sum aggregation. Recommend deduplicating or discounting confidence when the same proxy word supports more than one dimension.
- **`stable` and `delicate` are essentially unanchored** — the best available proxies (`secure`, `feeble`/`gentle`) either redundantly overlap with other V1 descriptors (`secure` ≈ `safe`) or point in inconsistent directions (`feeble` vs. `gentle`). These two descriptors should lean on Warriner/NRC (larger lexicons) rather than ANEW.

## Source Quality Note

This is a **primary source with full raw data** — the technical report itself contains the complete word-by-word rating tables (all-subjects, male-only, female-only), not just a description of methodology. Values for exact-match words were read directly from the "All Subjects" Table 1 in the PDF. Because the table is a dense multi-column layout recovered via PDF text extraction, there is a modest residual risk of row/column misalignment for any single cell; formatting was cross-checked for consistency across pages before use, and confidence scores for exact matches (0.45–0.70) reflect ANEW's small-to-moderate per-word sample sizes (n as low as 5–15 for some words) rather than extraction doubt. For the 13 descriptors requiring proxy words, the proxy selection itself is a judgment call and is the dominant source of uncertainty, reflected in the lower confidence scores (0.15–0.50) assigned throughout.
